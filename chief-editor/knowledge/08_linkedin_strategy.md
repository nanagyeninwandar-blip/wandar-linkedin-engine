# 8. LinkedIn Strategy Mastery

LinkedIn is Wandar's primary content channel. Ghost writers will produce most content here. The Chief Content Editor must know these four strategies better than any ghost writer so it can identify what was attempted, whether it was executed correctly, and exactly what needs to change.

---

## How to use this section

When a LinkedIn post arrives:
1. Identify which strategy it is (or was attempting to be)
2. Check execution against that strategy's template
3. Identify failure modes
4. Apply the correct level of intervention

A post that is well-written but executing the wrong strategy for its audience needs a strategy fix, not a polish. A post that has the right strategy but walks the hook back in the body needs a structural rewrite. Know the difference.

---

## Brandjacking

**What it is:**
Using a well-known brand's name in the hook to borrow their attention equity. The brand is the door in. Your insight is the room.

You are not attacking the brand. You are using their name recognition to pull the reader past "see more," then delivering a specific insight they could not get from the brand itself.

**Hook structure:**
- "What [Brand] gets right that most safari operators never copy."
- "Why [Brand] still wins bookings without running a single ad."
- "[Brand] built its reputation one thing at a time. Here is the playbook."
- "What [Brand]'s marketing budget can teach operators who have none."

**Post body must:**
- Validate the brand — do not tear it down
- Extract one transferable insight (not a list — one specific thing)
- Connect that insight directly to the safari operator's world with specifics
- End with an actionable implication or a soft CTA

**What the body must NOT do:**
- Just narrate what the brand does (that is a Wikipedia entry, not a post)
- Make the connection to operators vague or generic
- Attack or undermine the brand (kills credibility, creates defensive comments instead of engagement)

**Failure modes to catch:**
- Name-drop with no insight: the brand name appears in the hook but the body delivers nothing that required the brand's name to justify. Fix: either find the real transferable insight or rebuild the hook without the brand.
- Forced connection: the brand is famous for something that has no genuine relevance to safari operators. Fix: choose a brand whose story or method actually maps to the operator's world.
- Explanation mode: the body spends three paragraphs explaining the brand rather than extracting the insight. Fix: cut to the insight immediately after the hook.

**Editing test:**
Remove the brand name from the post. Does the insight still stand? If yes — the brand name was decoration. The connection is not real. Rebuild. If the insight collapses without the brand name — the brandjack is working.

## Namejacking

**What it is:**
Using a specific person's name — influencer, expert, founder, public figure — in the hook to ride their audience's attention and lend credibility through association.

The person is the hook. Your angle on their idea — specific to safari operators — is the value.

**Hook structure:**
- "[Person] says [specific idea]. Here is what that looks like for safari operators."
- "I disagree with [Person] on one thing. [State the disagreement in the hook]."
- "[Person] built [specific result] with [specific method]. Safari operators have the same opportunity."
- "[Person] is right about [X]. They could not have known about [Y]. That is where safari operators win."

**Post body must:**
- Reference the person's actual, verifiable idea — not a paraphrase so loose it becomes a misrepresentation
- Add a genuinely new angle that the named person could not have — Wandar's specific safari industry context
- Provide evidence or reasoning, not just assertion
- End with an operator-specific implication

**What the body must NOT do:**
- Misquote or exaggerate the person's position
- Simply agree with the person without adding anything (that is a comment, not a post)
- Use the name as bait and then deliver generic content that has nothing to do with the person's ideas
- Attack the person

**Failure modes to catch:**
- Inaccurate attribution: the post puts words or positions in the person's mouth they have not actually stated. Fix: verify the claim or remove the name and rebuild as a hot take with no attribution.
- No added angle: the post just summarises the person's idea and says "this applies to safari operators too." That is not insight. Fix: the ghost writer must find what the safari industry context adds that the named person's framework did not have.
- Name as bait: the hook uses the name to get the click, then the body never returns to the person's ideas at all. Fix: either bring the body back to the person's ideas or cut the name from the hook and build a different strategy.

**Editing test:**
Would the named person read this post and recognise their own ideas accurately represented? Would a follower of that person feel the connection between their ideas and safari operators is genuine? If either answer is no — fix or rebuild.

## Newsjacking

**What it is:**
Taking a real news story, market data point, or trending topic and inserting a specific safari operator perspective into it. You are riding existing conversation energy and delivering a viewpoint the news itself could not.

**Hook structure:**
- "[Specific news / stat]. Here is what that means for safari operators."
- "[News event] just happened. Most operators do not know it matters to their bookings."
- "Everyone is talking about [trend]. The safari industry version looks like this."
- "[Stat or data point]. Wandar has been watching this in real time."

**Post body must:**
- Connect the news to a specific operator behavior, challenge, or opportunity — not a general observation
- Add something the news itself did not say (the operator's angle, the lead gen implication, the timing reality)
- Be published quickly — newsjacking has a window

**What the body must NOT do:**
- Simply summarise the news and say "this is relevant to safari operators" without saying why or how
- Treat a 2-week-old news story as if it is breaking (the energy is gone)
- Make the connection so broad it could apply to any industry

**Failure modes to catch:**
- Stale news: the story broke more than 48-72 hours ago and the post has not been submitted yet. Fix: either find the angle that makes it evergreen (timeless insight from the news, not the news itself) or reject and advise the ghost writer to identify fresher news.
- No genuine connection: the operator angle is vague. "Safari bookings are affected by global travel trends" is not a newsjack — it is a generic observation. Fix: the ghost writer must identify the specific implication for a specific operator type (luxury operators, DMCs in Kenya, East Africa-focused operators).
- The insight is in the news: if everything the post says is in the news article itself, there is no added value. Fix: what does Wandar know about this from monitoring platforms in real time? What does the ghost writer know from the operator world that the reporter does not?

**Editing test:**
Would someone who already read the news article get something from this post they did not get from the article? If no — there is no reason to publish it. Rebuild the angle.

## Hot Takes

**What it is:**
A confident, specific, sometimes contrarian opinion stated plainly and defended with evidence or reasoning. Not manufactured controversy. Not rage bait. A real point of view that creates productive friction.

Niche hot takes outperform generic ones. A hot take that only safari operators will have a strong reaction to is better than one that anyone in any industry would scroll past.

**Hook structure:**
- "[Specific contrarian statement — no hedging]."
- "Most [operators / safari companies / lead gen advice] [wrong belief]. Here is what actually works."
- "[Common assumption]. That is wrong. Here is why."
- "[Confident claim that will split the room]."

**Post body must:**
- Defend the take with specific evidence, reasoning, or examples from the safari world
- Not walk the take back — this is the single most common ghost writer failure on this format
- End with a clear implication: what should the reader think or do differently?
- Create productive friction — the reader should feel compelled to agree, disagree, or share

**What the body must NOT do:**
- State the hot take in the hook then immediately qualify it into nothing ("of course, there are exceptions...")
- Be vague about what specifically is wrong or what specifically works instead
- Apply to any industry (a hot take about "the importance of speed in sales" is not a safari industry hot take)
- Be mean-spirited or personal

**Failure modes to catch:**
- The walk-back: the hook is confident, the body immediately hedges. This is the ghost writer losing nerve. It kills the engagement signal. Fix: cut every qualification from the body. If the take cannot survive without hedging, the take is wrong — rebuild it as something the ghost writer can defend.
- The obvious take: nobody would disagree. A hot take that generates no friction generates no reach. Fix: sharpen the take. What is the specific thing operators believe that Wandar knows from experience is wrong?
- The generic take: "speed matters in sales" is not a safari industry hot take. Fix: make it specific to the safari operator world. Name the behavior, the platform, the moment where speed matters and most operators miss it.
- The rant: confident but no reasoning, no evidence, no implication. It reads as opinion, not insight. Fix: add one specific piece of evidence or one specific example from the safari world.

**Editing test:**
Read the hook and then the body. Did the body strengthen the take or soften it? If softer — rewrite the body. Would a safari operator read this and feel they need to respond (agree or disagree)? If indifferent — the take is not sharp enough.

## LinkedIn Technical Rules (apply to all four strategies)

These are not content quality issues. They are algorithm issues. A ghost writer can produce a great post and still fail technically.

**Hook (lines 1-3):**
LinkedIn cuts the post at approximately 210 characters (roughly 2-3 lines). Everything before that cut must stand alone as a reason to read more. The hook is not setup. The hook is not context. The hook is the sharpest line in the post. If the sharpest line is not in the first 3 lines — move it there.

**Line breaks:**
One idea per line. Blank line between paragraphs. Dense paragraphs kill reach on LinkedIn. A ghost writer who writes in paragraphs on LinkedIn needs a structural rewrite, not a light edit.

**External links:**
Never in the post body. LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with outbound links in the body. If there is a link, it goes in the first comment. The post ends with "link in the first comment" (or similar).

**Hashtags:**
3-5 maximum. Niche beats broad.
- Useful: #safarioperators #safaribusiness #touroperators #safarileadgen
- Not useful: #travel #marketing #business #success

If a ghost writer has put 15 hashtags — cut to 5. If they have used only broad hashtags — replace with niche ones.

**Post length:**
Varies by strategy. Hot takes can be short (5-10 lines). Newsjacking and Namejacking typically need more body to justify the hook (15-25 lines). There is no fixed length — judge by whether every line is earning its place.

**Audience calibration:**
If the post is aimed at a specific segment (operators only vs. broader LinkedIn audience), the voice and vocabulary must match. A post aimed at investors should not be full of safari operator vocabulary. A post aimed at operators should not sound like a tech pitch. Catch this in Channel Calibration (Step 3).

## Combining Strategies

The best LinkedIn posts often combine two strategies. This is not a rule — it is a pattern to recognise and occasionally encourage.

Common combinations:
- Newsjacking + Hot take: Use the news as evidence for a contrarian position. The news is the proof. The hot take is the angle.
- Namejacking + Hot take: Agree with a named person on the surface, then disagree on the safari-specific implication.
- Brandjacking + Newsjacking: A brand's recent news is the hook, the operator insight is the body.

When a ghost writer has attempted a combination:
- Identify both strategies
- Check whether both are being executed or whether one is cannibalising the other
- A post that tries to be both a brandjack and a hot take but commits to neither ends up as neither — it needs a decision
