# 9. Content Channel Intelligence

What the Chief Content Editor must know to judge correctly on each of Wandar's five content channels. Not writing instructions — judgment criteria. What does perfect execution look like? What are the failure modes specific to this channel?

---

## LinkedIn

Already covered in full in `knowledge/08_linkedin_strategy.md` and `knowledge/03_distribution_channels.md`.

Additional judgment criteria beyond Section 8:

**Audience variants and what changes:**

| Audience | Voice shift | What to watch for |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Safari operators / DMCs | Practical, grounded, operator-insider | Safari vocabulary, WhatsApp reality, referral-based mindset |
| Investors / VCs | Market size, defensibility, growth signal | Remove operator jargon. Add market context. |
| Travel media / journalists | Story angle, newsworthiness, source quality | Is there a story here, not just a statement? |
| Adjacent founders | Founder-to-founder honesty, the build reality | Authenticity over polish. Real problems, not vision decks. |
| Aspiring guides / industry newcomers | Access, education, insider knowledge | Do not condescend. Treat as intelligent but new. |

**Voice calibration:**
When the audience is operators: the voice is a founder who understands their world specifically. Not a software marketer. Not a travel blogger. Someone who knows what losing a lead to a competitor on a Tuesday morning feels like.

When the audience is broader (investors, media): the voice is still direct and grounded, but the vocabulary shifts. No WhatsApp references. No "inbound." Frame in market terms.

**The pass criteria for LinkedIn:**
- Hook earns the "see more" click
- Strategy is identifiable and executed correctly
- No external link in post body
- Paragraphs are broken into single-idea lines
- Voice matches the audience
- Specific to safari operators (or the specific audience) — not generic
- No em dashes, no AI phrasing

## Landing Page

**Audience:** ICP only. Luxury safari operators and DMC principals who are actively evaluating Wandar. They are skeptical of software. They have been pitched by generic tools before. They will leave in seconds if the copy does not immediately signal that someone understands their world.

**What makes a landing page section pass:**

- Hero (above the fold): One headline. One subheadline. One CTA. Nothing else. The headline must communicate what Wandar does and why it matters to an operator in one sentence. The subheadline supports — it does not repeat.
- Problem section: States the problem the operator already feels. Does not educate — recognises. The operator reads this and thinks "yes, that is exactly it." If they have to be convinced the problem exists: rewrite.
- Solution section: How Wandar works in 3 steps. Clear, specific, jargon-free. Not "AI-powered social listening" — "finds travelers who post their safari budget on Reddit before your competitors see it."
- Proof section: Specific platform behaviors, real examples, mechanics of intent scoring. Not just testimonials. Operators need to believe the mechanism works, not just that others liked it.
- CTA sections: One CTA per section. Never two competing actions. The CTA language is specific: "See how it works" not "Learn more."

**Failure modes:**
- Headline that could apply to any SaaS product: reject or rebuild
- "Leveraging AI" or any SaaS vocabulary: remove and replace with the specific mechanic
- Multiple CTAs on the same section competing for attention: remove all but one
- Problem section that explains the problem rather than recognising it: restructure so the operator feels seen, not educated
- Any em dash: remove — this is the channel where operators are most likely evaluating with full attention

**The pass criteria for landing page:**
- Headline works without the subheadline
- Problem section makes an operator feel understood
- No SaaS vocabulary
- One CTA per section, specific action language
- No em dashes anywhere
- Every sentence earns its place — nothing present for filler

## Medium

**Audience:** Operators searching for safari lead gen solutions (via Google or Medium search), travel industry professionals, adjacent founders, media, investors. Broader than LinkedIn — must be more accessible.

**What makes a Medium article pass:**

- Headline: Must match how the reader searches, not what the writer finds clever. "How safari operators find high-intent leads before competitors" performs better than "The invisible pipeline in plain sight." Test: would an operator type this headline into Google?
- First paragraph: Earns the read. Same hook logic as LinkedIn but with more space. The reader must know by the end of the first paragraph exactly what they are going to learn and why it matters.
- Structure: Clear subheadings that work as a scannable outline. A reader who only reads subheadings should understand the shape of the argument.
- AEO phrasing: Clean, quotable sentences. Write answers, not just content. An LLM should be able to extract a direct answer to a specific question from this article. Vague claims do not get cited.
- Length: 1,500-3,000 words for authority pieces. Not padded — earned. Every section has a job.
- Specificity: The articles that earn LLM citation name platforms, behaviors, timelines, and mechanisms. "Safari travelers post on Reddit" is not citable. "Safari travelers on Reddit typically share budget, destination, travel dates, and group size in a single post" is citable.

**Failure modes:**
- Clever headline that does not match search intent: replace with a direct headline
- Generic opening ("In today's competitive safari market...") that delays the value: cut and start with the insight
- Body that makes broad claims without specifics: identify every vague claim and replace with a named platform, behavior, or mechanic
- No clear subheading structure: add subheadings that work as a scannable outline
- Passive voice throughout: rewrite active
- Missing a clear CTA or next step: add one at the end (email sign-up, Wandar trial, related article)

**The pass criteria for Medium:**
- Headline matches search intent
- First paragraph earns the read
- Subheadings tell the story on their own
- At least one citable, specific, extractable sentence per section
- No generic claims — every claim has a specific attached
- CTA at the end is single and clear

## Substack

**Audience:** Opted-in subscribers — operators and industry people who already know Wandar exists. Warm audience. They signed up because they want the insider view.

**What this channel is for:**
Building the relationship between Wandar and the operator community. Positioning Wandar's founder voice as a trusted source of safari industry intelligence. Not for conversion — for trust and retention.

**What makes a Substack post pass:**

- Subject line: This is the email subject, not a blog title. It must earn an open. It works like a LinkedIn hook — specific, curious, not generic. "The lead your competitors saw this week that you didn't" is a subject line. "Safari lead generation insights" is not.
- Preheader text: Extends the subject line. Does not repeat it. Treats the preheader as the second hook.
- Body: More personal than Medium. More conversational. The reader is an insider — write for them as such. Share something they cannot get from a public blog post.
- One CTA: At the end. Specific action. Not competing with the content.
- Length: 400-800 words typically. Long enough to be worth reading. Short enough to respect the inbox.
- Voice: Founder-to-operator. Direct, grounded, no polish for the sake of polish. The reader should feel like they are getting the unfiltered view.

**Failure modes:**
- Subject line that reads like a blog title: replace with an email hook
- Generic body that could be published anywhere: it must feel exclusive, like an inside view
- Multiple CTAs: remove all but one
- Over-polished tone that sounds like a press release: strip back to founder voice
- Longer than it needs to be: cut until every sentence is earning its place

**The pass criteria for Substack:**
- Subject line earns the open (would an operator open this at 8am on a Monday?)
- Preheader extends the hook, does not repeat it
- Body feels personal and inside — not broadcast
- One CTA, specific
- Voice is founder-to-operator, not brand-to-audience

## Newsletter (planned)

Until the newsletter is fully operational, apply the same standards as Substack.

When live and fully operational, additional criteria will apply:
- Segmentation: is this content targeted to the right operator segment? Content for luxury operators should not be sent to mid-market operators with different budgets and expectations.
- Automation sequences: onboarding emails, nurture sequences, and re-engagement emails each have different jobs. Identify the email's job before judging it.
- Subject line A/B logic: if two subject line options are submitted, judge both against the same criteria and recommend the stronger one with reasoning.

## Operator Response Content (monitoring platforms)

When ghost writers draft responses for operators to post on Reddit, TripAdvisor, Quora, or similar platforms — this is a different content type. Apply monitoring platform intelligence from `knowledge/04_monitoring_platform_intelligence.md`, not content channel intelligence.

The core principle: it must read as a native, knowledgeable person helping — not as marketing. If it reads like marketing in any way: reject or rebuild.
