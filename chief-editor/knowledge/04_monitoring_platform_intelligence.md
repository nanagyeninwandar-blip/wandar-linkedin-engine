# 4. Monitoring Platform Intelligence

These are the 9 platforms Wandar's product monitors to find high-intent safari travelers. They are not Wandar's content channels.

This section exists for one purpose: when ghost writers draft operator response content for these platforms (responses to traveler threads, forum replies, etc.), the Chief Content Editor needs to know what good looks like on each platform so it can judge correctly.

---

**Reddit**

What travelers do here: Post detailed, explicit plans. Budget, destination, dates, group size. Subreddits: r/safari, r/travel, r/AfricaTravel.
Example post: "Planning a Kenya/Tanzania trip for October 2025, family of 4, budget around $25k total. Which operator should I use?"

What a good operator response looks like:
- Directly answers the question asked — do not pivot to a pitch
- Specific to the traveler's stated situation (references their budget, dates, destination)
- No promotional language — sounds like a knowledgeable person, not a brand
- No headers or heavy formatting — paragraph form only
- Links only when genuinely helpful and not self-serving

What fails on Reddit:
- Any promotional language (immediate downvotes, destroys credibility)
- Generic advice that ignores the traveler's specifics
- Formatting that looks corporate (bullet lists, bold headers)
- Starting with the operator's company name

Judgment test: Read it as a Reddit user who has no idea who wrote it. Does it sound like a real knowledgeable person helping? Or does it sound like marketing? If marketing — reject or rebuild.

---

**TripAdvisor**

What travelers do here: Ask for operator recommendations in forums. Post detailed trip planning questions.
Example post: "Looking for a luxury tented camp in the Serengeti for our anniversary — any recommendations?"

What a good operator response looks like:
- Conversational, expert tone
- Specific to what was asked (destination, budget, style of travel)
- Evidence of genuine knowledge (names parks, camps, seasons correctly)
- No hard sell — the expertise is the pitch

What fails:
- Generic recommendations that could apply to any safari
- Promotional language or pushing a specific operator without context
- Ignoring the specifics of what was asked

---

**Quora**

What travelers do here: Ask structured questions.
Examples: "What is the best time to visit the Masai Mara?" "How do I find a reputable Tanzania safari operator?"

What a good operator response looks like:
- Long-form, structured, expert
- Directly answers the question in the first sentence
- Specific — names parks, seasons, behaviors
- AEO-optimized: clean, quotable sentences that LLMs can extract
- Helpful without being promotional

What fails:
- Vague answers that do not commit to specifics
- Promotional language
- Not answering the question before adding context

---

**Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, Pinterest, Bluesky**

Wandar monitors these for lead signals. Operator response content for these platforms is rare. If submitted, apply the same core principle: native, genuinely helpful, non-promotional. Refer to the platform's community norms before judging.
