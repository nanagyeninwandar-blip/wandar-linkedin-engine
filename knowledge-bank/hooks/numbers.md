# Number Hooks

A specific number does two things: signals research, and creates a concrete mental image. Vague numbers kill the effect. Exact numbers earn it.

**Rule:** The number must be real or a real observation framed as such. Never fabricate statistics. Frame estimates honestly.

---

## Proven patterns (from 0B-linkedin-creator.md)

**The review gap:**
"23 of their last 30 TripAdvisor reviews mentioned their head guide by name. He's nowhere on their website."

Why it works: 23 of 30 is specific enough to be credible, concrete enough to visualize.

---

**The response window:**
"The booking window is hours to a few days, not weeks. Every hour without a response is a window a competitor can climb through."

Why it works: Reframes "fast response" from advice to math.

---

**The platform count:**
"Wandar monitors 9+ platforms continuously. Every minute, every hour, every day."

Why it works: Specificity (9+) + rhythm (every minute/hour/day) = authority.

---

**The price signal:**
"$1,500/night guests don't book on impulse. They research for months. Then they find the operator who showed up in the right conversation."

Why it works: Dollar figure anchors the audience (operators know their price point immediately).

---

## Construction formula

1. Find a real number in your observations, research, or Wandar data
2. State it before the context — let the number land first
3. Then explain what it means

Bad: "Many safari operators lose bookings because they respond too slowly."
Good: "47% of safari inquiries go unanswered in the first 24 hours. That's not a marketing problem. That's a response problem."

Note: If you use a percentage, source it or frame it as an observation pattern.

## Number sources for Wandar posts

- TripAdvisor review counts (real, verifiable)
- Reddit thread statistics (searchable)
- Wandar platform monitoring count (9+)
- Pricing tiers ($299/month, $599/month, $500-$2,000/night)
- Response time windows (hours, not days)
- Booking lead times (18 months in advance for premium)
