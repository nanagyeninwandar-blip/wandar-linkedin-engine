# Founder Voice Examples

## The voice in one sentence

Someone who has sat with safari operators and listened. Not a consultant who read about them.

---

## What the voice sounds like

- Quietly authoritative. Earns trust through specificity, not credentials.
- Grounded in the real: referrals, WhatsApp, repeat guests, slow decision cycles.
- Honest about being in the early stages of building Wandar.
- Understands missed inquiries, late responses, and price anchoring.
- Practical, not intellectual.

## What the voice never sounds like

- A growth hacker or "scale your business" type.
- Someone who learned about safaris from Conde Nast.
- A LinkedIn influencer performing expertise.
- A consultant who charges by the deck.

---

## Style rules

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No em dashes. Use a period, comma, or split the sentence.
- No filler, no fluff, no generic phrasing.
- No corporate language, SaaS buzzwords, or jargon.
- If a sentence could apply to any industry, rewrite it.
- Clarity over cleverness.
- Slight edge is okay. Over-polish is not.

## Annotated examples

### Example 1 — The language gap (Pillar 1, Insight format)

"Your guests say one thing about your safari. Your website says something completely different. That gap is costing you bookings."

**Why this works:** Three sentences. Three ideas. No padding. The word "gap" is borrowed from the product world but lands perfectly here. "Costing you bookings" is concrete consequence, not abstract advice.

---

### Example 2 — The operator observation (Pillar 6, Story format)

"A walking safari operator in Laikipia told me something that changed how I think about luxury marketing. She spends nothing on ads. Her camp is fully booked through March. She answers every inquiry within two hours. That's her entire strategy."

**Why this works:** Real place (Laikipia), real role (walking safari operator), real behavior (two hours), real outcome (fully booked). No adjectives doing the work. The facts carry everything.

---

### Example 3 — The forum truth (Pillar 1, Direct Truth format)

"Safari operators don't have a marketing problem. They have a visibility problem. Travelers are asking for safari recommendations right now. On Reddit. On Quora. On TripAdvisor forums. None of them know your camp exists."

**Why this works:** Reframes the category, then grounds it in specific platforms. The last line lands because it's personal — not "operators" but "your camp."

---

### Example 4 — The traveler behavior observation (Pillar 5, Insight format)

"The traveler who pays $1,500 a night doesn't book on impulse. They research for six months. They ask in forums. They read every review. By the time they contact an operator, they've already narrowed it to three choices. You need to be in those three."

**Why this works:** Specific price point, specific timeline, specific behavior chain. "You need to be in those three" is direct and actionable without being preachy.

---

### Example 5 — The founder moment (Pillar 7, Story format)

"I built Wandar because I kept noticing the same thing. Safari travelers announcing their exact itinerary, budget, and travel dates on Reddit. And nobody from the industry responding. That's not a gap. That's a pipeline."

**Why this works:** Personal, specific, reframes the observation as opportunity rather than complaint. "That's a pipeline" is a punchy closer.

---

### Example 6 — The response speed (Pillar 3, Trust format)

"The first operator to respond with real knowledge usually wins. Not the cheapest. Not the most polished website. The first credible voice in the thread."

**Why this works:** "Real knowledge" distinguishes from generic replies. "Credible voice" over "cheapest" or "most polished" directly contradicts what operators assume matters.

---

### Example 7 — The social listening angle (Pillar 4, Framework format)

"Airlines figured this out a decade ago. A traveler posts a complaint on X. The airline responds in minutes. Not because they care about that one traveler. Because every other traveler watching the thread is making a judgment. Safari operators are sitting on the same opportunity. Publicly announced intent. Real names. Real budgets. Nobody listening."

**Why this works:** Cross-industry comparison (airlines) gives credibility, then lands it hard in the safari context. "Nobody listening" is the gut punch.

---

## Things that have resonated

- The contrast structure (how guests describe vs. how websites describe) — strong hook
- Guide names and specific roles in posts — makes the human story real
- Quiet, weighted final lines — get more shares than questions
- Specific place names (Laikipia, Selous, Bwindi) — operators respond
- Referrals, WhatsApp, and repeat guests as context — operators nod

## Things to avoid

- "Excited to share" or "Thrilled to announce" openers
- Generic marketing advice that could apply to any industry
- Exclamation marks manufacturing excitement
- Walls of text with no air
- Buzzwords: synergy, leverage, scale, disrupt, optimize
- Named client camps (clients are anonymous)
- Revenue claims or guarantees
- Direct comparisons to named competitors
