# Narrative Frameworks

## Framework 1 — The Language Gap

**Core idea:** What operators say about themselves vs. what guests actually say. The gap is costing bookings.

**Structure:**
1. State what the operator says (marketing language)
2. State what the guest says (human language)
3. Name the gap
4. Connect to the consequence (missed bookings, wrong guests)

**Best for:** Pillar 1, Growth/Authority bucket, Counterintuitive or Contrast format

**Example close:** "The words on your website are yours. The words in a booking decision are your guests'. The gap between them is where you're losing people."

---

## Framework 2 — The Visibility Window

**Core idea:** The booking decision happens early — before operators know it's happening. By the time the inquiry arrives, the shortlist is already formed.

**Structure:**
1. Show when travelers actually start deciding (much earlier than operators think)
2. Name where it happens (forums, communities — not Google)
3. Show who wins (whoever showed up in that conversation)
4. Name the cost of arriving late (shortlist already closed)

**Best for:** Pillar 1 + 2, Growth bucket, Newsjacking or Hot Take

---

## Framework 3 — The First Voice Wins

**Core idea:** In high-trust purchases, the first credible knowledgeable voice usually wins the relationship.

**Structure:**
1. Name the moment the decision really starts (a forum post, a Reddit thread)
2. Show what the traveler is looking for (expertise, not a sales pitch)
3. Name what winning looks like (knowledge, speed, specificity)
4. Land the implication (most operators aren't in that conversation at all)

**Best for:** Pillar 3, Authority bucket, Framework format

---

## Framework 4 — The Cross-Industry Mirror

**Core idea:** An adjacent industry figured this out years ago. Safari operators haven't yet.

**Structure:**
1. Name the other industry (airlines, hotels, luxury retail)
2. Show what they do (social listening, real-time response, community engagement)
3. Mirror it to safari (same opportunity, same traveler behavior, nobody doing it)
4. Name the gap safari operators can own

**Best for:** Pillar 4, Authority bucket, Framework format

**Known examples:** Airlines and X complaints, hotels and TripAdvisor management, luxury brands and community engagement

---

## Framework 5 — The Buyer Reality vs. Operator Assumption

**Core idea:** Operators make assumptions about what $1,500/night guests want. The reality is usually different.

**Structure:**
1. State the assumption ("luxury travelers want X")
2. State the reality ("they actually want Y")
3. Show why the difference matters
4. Connect to what operators should do differently

**Best for:** Pillar 5, Authority bucket, Contrast format

---

## Framework 6 — The Compounding Trust Signal

**Core idea:** Trust doesn't form at the point of inquiry. It forms in layers, long before contact.

**Structure:**
1. Show the trust formation chain (community recommendation > review quality > response speed > expertise)
2. Name at which point most operators start paying attention (too late — at inquiry)
3. Show where the real trust work happens (much earlier)
4. Land: operators who build trust before inquiry convert more

**Best for:** Pillar 3, Authority bucket, Framework or Insight format
